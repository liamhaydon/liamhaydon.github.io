Contoso Retail Analytics

Contoso is a fictional global electronics retailer with four years (2021–2024) of sales from stores and an online shop. I built a Power BI report that answers the questions a sales manager would ask, with clear charts and visualizations per question.

The data

How it's organized

The data is set up as a star schema. factSales sits in the center with one row per item sold on an order, and links to three dimension tables through ProductKey, CustomerKey and StoreKey.

  • dimProduct: what was sold (brand, category, color, list price and cost)
  • dimCustomer: who bought it (age, gender, city and country)
  • dimStore: where it sold, including the online store (country, size, open and close dates)

One product, customer or store can appear in many sales. Filters flow from the dimensions into the sales table, so picking a category or country instantly updates every number in the report.

Under the hood

The SQL behind it

Two of the report's answers, written as SQL queries against the same star schema. Click to expand.

04 Which product categories are the most profitable? 
SELECT
    p.CategoryName,
    SUM(s.Quantity * (s.NetPrice - s.UnitCost)) AS Profit
FROM factSales AS s
JOIN dimProduct AS p
    ON s.ProductKey = p.ProductKey
GROUP BY p.CategoryName
ORDER BY Profit DESC;
  • JOIN connects each sale to its product, so we know its category.
  • Profit for each sale = quantity × (selling price − cost), and SUM adds it up.
  • GROUP BY gives one total per category, and ORDER BY … DESC puts the most profitable first.
05 Who are our repeat customers? 
SELECT
    c.GivenName,
    c.Surname,
    COUNT(DISTINCT s.OrderKey)   AS Orders,
    SUM(s.Quantity * s.NetPrice) AS Revenue
FROM factSales AS s
JOIN dimCustomer AS c
    ON s.CustomerKey = c.CustomerKey
GROUP BY c.CustomerKey, c.GivenName, c.Surname
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT s.OrderKey) > 1
ORDER BY Orders DESC, Revenue DESC;
  • JOIN adds each customer's name to their sales.
  • COUNT(DISTINCT OrderKey) counts real orders. One order can have several product lines, so a plain count would overcount.
  • HAVING … > 1 keeps only customers with more than one order. It runs after grouping, which is why it isn't WHERE.
Reflection

What I learned

Start with the question

Each page answers one business question. Starting there kept the report focused instead of becoming a wall of charts.

Know your data

Understanding how the tables connect made it much easier to build accurate visuals and trust the numbers.

Keep it simple

One chart, one takeaway. Clear titles and a few key numbers say more than a crowded dashboard.
